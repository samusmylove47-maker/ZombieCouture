## 2. The master render (the long job)

The farm renders from a frozen copy of the tree so edits cannot reach a running render: `tools/snap.sh` (runs the smoke gate, copies `web/`, `tools/`, `assets/`, `data/timing.json`, `data/shots.json` to a folder `zc_snap` next to the repository). `tools/master_driver.sh` loops: it reads `data/ready.txt` (shot ids, one per line or comma separated), runs `tools/render_all.sh master --foreground --only <ids>` from the snapshot into `out/frames/master`, sleeps, and reads the list again. Frames that exist and are intact are skipped, so a pass only costs what is missing.

```
nohup tools/master_driver.sh >> out/master_driver.log 2>&1 &     # start (it resumes)
python3 tools/farm.py --status --out out/frames/master           # progress, s/frame, eta
tail out/master_driver.log                                       # which pass is running
touch out/master_driver.stop                                     # stop after the current pass
kill -TERM <farm pid>                                            # stop now; the current frame finishes
```

State on 2026-10-01 02:05: **the master render is complete and corrected.** `out/frames/master` holds the first render (4,248 frames, finished 2026-09-30 21:56); the neon-sign flicker found afterwards (section 4) meant that the 1,127 frames that show a sign were rendered again, from `zc_fix` (a copy of the snapshot plus the one-line fix), into `out/frames/master2` (the other 3,121 frames are hard links to the originals, made with `cp -al` before the shown frames were removed; 290 of the 1,127 came out bit-identical because the sign did not fight in them). `master2` is the film: the release encodes, the widescreen hook and the pictures for the director's page come from it. The pass took 23:45 to 02:00 on the two cores (about 7 s a frame). First render, in detail: Pass 1 (the first 43 shots, 2,109 frames, intro to pre-chorus 2, 0:00 to 1:28) finished at 17:07, including the 29 `noBite` frames (574 to 602) that the supervisor's extra pass re-rendered; pass 2 (the other 38 shots: chorus 2, bridge, finale, outro, card; 2,139 frames) ran from 17:07 to 21:56. Undisturbed the farm ran at 8.5 to 10 s/frame on the second half (the bridge and finale shots are heavier than the first half's 6.5 to 7.5); 12 to 17 s/frame while encodes or review renders shared the two cores. Every test render on the same two cores slows the farm, so look at frames rather than render new ones. Everything below this paragraph about driving a running render is kept for the next film.

Rules while it runs:

* Never change the behaviour of a shot that already has frames (code, args, timing, sets): its frames would not match the rest. If a shot must change, delete its frames (`out/frames/master/f_<nnnnn>.jpg`, number = round(t x 24), t from `data/shots.json`) and let the driver render it again.
* Shots with no frames yet may still be edited: run `node web/film/smoke.mjs data/shots.json`, then `tools/snap.sh`. The next pass uses the new snapshot.
* If `data/shots.json` is regenerated, the first shots must come out identical (`diff` against a copy); a shifted cut moves every later frame number.

## 3. After the frames exist

1. `python3 tools/farm.py --status --out out/frames/master` says done, no `missing.txt`.
2. QA: `python3 tools/qa_frames.py out/frames/master --shots data/shots.json` (blank, frozen, jump, flash), `python3 tools/nanprobe.py` style checks if a frame looks corrupted, then contact sheets (`tools/sheet.py out.jpg 6 320 frames...`) and a real look at every shot's first, middle and last frame.
3. A quick look at any finished stretch while the rest renders: `tools/encode_preview.sh out/frames/master data/song.mp3 <t0> <t1> out/preview.mp4` (720p, the matching piece of the song; every frame of the range must exist).
   The release encode: `tools/encode_master.sh out/frames/master data/song.mp3 out/zombie-couture_master.mp4` (BT.709, audio padded to 177 s, `mux_check` runs at the end; also writes a 720p copy for sending around). The felt texture is expensive, so the defaults were set from measurements (2026-09-30, 72-frame samples of three stretches, preset medium): 1080p crf 16 is 42 to 66 Mbps (about 1 GB for the film), crf 20 is 20 to 35 Mbps (about 550 MB, visually the same as the JPEG frames at 100 %), crf 24 is 7 to 16 Mbps (about 240 MB, fine fibres start to smooth), crf 28 is clearly soft; 720p crf 24 is 2.4 to 6 Mbps (about 85 MB). So: 1080p crf 20, 720p crf 24, the hooks crf 25 (about 13 Mbps, 20 to 30 MB).
4. Hooks: the window is chorus 1, shots `c1_03` to `c1_10`, frames 899 to 1261 (363 frames, 15.125 s, the four chorus lines). Widescreen: `python3 tools/hook_tag.py` (the maker tag, `out/hook_tag.png`), then `tools/encode_hook169.sh out/frames/master data/song.mp3 37.4583333 15.125 out/hook_tag.png out/hook_15s_16x9.mp4` (no new render). Vertical: `tools/render_all.sh short --from 37.45 --to 52.55 --q "short=/data/short.json"`, then `tools/encode_short.sh out/frames/short data/song.mp3 37.4583333 15.125 out/hook_vertical_9x16.mp4`.

Frames are deterministic, so unchanged frames stay bit-identical and nothing else needs to be looked at twice.

### Delivery to the director's computer

The chat upload limit is 30 MiB, so the master goes through the desktop link instead (the session is linked to the director's Windows PC, whose connected folder is the Videos folder; the files go into a subfolder named Zombie Couture). Split the file (`split -b 19000000 -d -a 3 file <outputs folder>/<name>/p.`), `device_commit_files` the parts (`stagedPath` inside the workspace's outputs folder, at most 20 MB per file and 100 MB per call) into a `_parts` subfolder, then `device_bash` runs `cat _parts/p.* > <final>.mp4` and compares `sha256sum` with the original. Two things found when testing it: the commit step stamps a provenance manifest (an extra `uuid` box of about 6 KB) into any file that starts like an MP4, so give the first part a one-byte prefix (`printf '\0' | cat - p.000 > p.000x`, and `tail -c +2` it on the device) when the file must stay byte-exact; and deleting needs `device_request_delete_permission` (otherwise move the leftover parts into `_to_delete`). Last time the director kept a 1080p file, a 15 s 16:9 hook clip, a vertical hook clip, `thumbnail.jpg`, `captions.srt` and `youtube_kit.md` in one folder; the Zombie Couture folder mirrors that.

### The vertical Short

`tools/render_all.sh short --from 37.45 --to 52.55 --q "short=/data/short.json"` (frames 899 to 1261, 363 frames, shots `c1_03` to `c1_10`, the four chorus lines "Pastel pink and lace" to "Zombie couture, zombie couture"; a frame belongs to a shot when its time is at or after the shot's start, so the window starts at frame 899, not 898). Portrait keeps the landscape picture's width by default, which leaves subjects small; `web/film/film.js` therefore reads `?pz=` (width factor) and `?short=<json>` (per shot `pz`, `yaw`, `tilt` in degrees, `dx` in metres) and only in portrait, so landscape frames are untouched. `data/short.json` holds the tuned numbers. Check the first, middle and last frame of every shot before the long render, and the lines of type: `titleStitch` (`c1_09`) scales its embroidered title to 1.15 in portrait (1.5 in landscape) because at 1.5 the narrow frame cut COUTURE off at both sides (found only when the finished Short was looked at frame by frame; the same lesson as for the signs in section 4).

## 4. Known soft spots (none is a defect that stops the release)

* Early shots that are already rendered and would need their frames deleted to change: `beansCU` (label legibility), `cloudyEyes`, `pipBack`, `scratchCU`; `pipReact` is unused.
* Fixed after the first frames came out (second case): `noBite` (v1_08). The first camera stood behind Pip, whose huge head hid the zombie's lean and hat tip. The camera now stands on the concourse over the zombie's shoulder; its 69 frames (534 to 602) were moved to `out/old_frames/noBite_v1/` and rendered again. A third change followed: the hat-tip arm crossed Pip's eye in the last half second, so from the word "bite." (frame 574) the shot now cuts to a front view of the zombie's face (frames 574 to 602 moved to `out/old_frames/noBite_v2/`; frames 534 to 573 are unchanged). The farm renders them in its extra pass at the end of pass 1. A farm worker builds its list of missing frames when it starts, so frames removed from a running render are picked up at its next restart (or the next pass).
* Fixed after the first frames came out: `pipListen` (v1_03) had a one-frame pop at 13.71 s. The camera started on the far side of the door module's dark wall, which filled the right of the frame and disappeared when the camera crossed it (single-sided geometry). The push-in now stays on one side (`dist` 2.68 to 2.42 m) and the 26 frames were rendered again. Lesson for any shot: look at the middle of a camera move, not only its ends; `qa_frames.py` finds such pops as unplanned jumps (the intro's vending-machine flicker shows up there too, by design). Look at them in the real frames before deciding whether any is worth a re-render.
* Fixed after the master was rendered (2026-09-30, found in the vertical Short, where the camera sits close under a sign): the five neon sign planes in `web/set.js` lay exactly on the wall's front face (extrude depth 0.6 plus bevel 0.08 from z = -12 is -11.32, the plane's z), so at oblique angles the two surfaces z-fought, frame by frame: the sign flickered, half-drawn or riddled with a grid of dropouts (worst in `c1_06`, the camera orbiting under the "GLOSS BAR" sign). The fix is `polygonOffset` on the sign material: depth only, nothing moves, so every frame without a sign is identical to before. `tools/signscan.py` lists the frames where a sign is on screen (1,127 of 4,248 in the film, 274 of the Short's 363, none in the slider shot); exactly those were rendered again from a snapshot with the fix and the encodes made again. Frames are deterministic (a second render of the same frame is bit-identical), so the glitch was in the content and not in the render. `tools/zfight_audit.py` is the static check: it lists pairs of triangles of different meshes that lie in one plane and overlap, without rendering anything. What it still lists is harmless (same-colour faces, faces turned away from the camera, floor decals 2 mm apart that read clean in every rosette frame looked at). Lesson: look at magnified or odd-angle crops of background signage and decals, not only at the middle of the frame, and keep surfaces that must sit on another one a few millimetres off it or give them a polygon offset.
* Faces are small in wide shots; the performance lives in the close-ups and the mouth rig.
* Chorus shot lengths average 1.9 to 2.4 s against the 1.7 to 1.9 s target (the lyric lines are long held notes).
* Held notes: the cut rule is "no plain cut inside a held word (over 0.9 s) except on a section downbeat or where the storyboard says `"held": "ok"`" (`o_01b`: a bar-line cut in the aerial outro, no singer on screen). The finale cuts `f_02` and `f_03` sit on the words "dresses" and "for".
